Slope, Width, and the Singular Series

Sequel to Strip Arithmetic I — The Goldbach Diagonal
One residue count runs the whole family. It produces min(n+1, p) in C(n), and it produces the Goldbach correction ∏(p−1)/(p−2). Same rule, two slopes.

§1 · The claimC(n) is a strip width

A block $\{M, M+1, \dots, M+n\}$ is $n+1$ adjacent diagonals of the coprimality lattice. The gap diagonal identity is the width-1 case. Block-coprime density $C(n)$ is the general width case. Turn the slope from $+1$ to $-1$ and the same machinery lands on Goldbach.

Most of what follows is one walk over a strip: the set of lattice points $(r, M)$ satisfying

$$ s \;\le\; b\,r - a\,M \;<\; s + w $$

for a slope $a/b$, an offset $s$, and a width $w$. The block case is the exception, and it is worth stating precisely because getting it wrong is easy: $C(n)$ is not the coprime density inside a diagonal strip. The block $\{M,\dots,M+n\}$ runs along $M$, and the condition is a conjunction — the point $r$ must be coprime to every member:

$$ \gcd(r,\,M+j) = 1 \quad \text{for all } j = 0,\dots,n $$

A vertical band of $n+1$ columns with an AND across it, not an average within a diagonal strip. The density of cells surviving that AND is $D(n)$; the average coprime density inside a width-$(n{+}1)$ diagonal strip is a different and much larger number. Band mode below measures the first. This is the same condition Strip Arithmetic I applies in its block n control, walked over the whole lattice here instead of along a single strip.

The rule underneath

Fix a finite set of integer offsets $\mathcal{H}$. For each prime $p$, let

$$ \nu_p(\mathcal{H}) \;=\; \#\{\, h \bmod p \;:\; h \in \mathcal{H} \,\} $$

be the number of distinct residue classes $\mathcal{H}$ occupies mod $p$. That single count drives everything. Where two offsets collide mod $p$, $\nu_p$ drops, and the local factor relaxes.

Slope +1 — the block

$\mathcal{H} = \{0,1,\dots,n\}$. Consecutive integers, so by pigeonhole $\nu_p = \min(n+1, p)$ — every class is hit once $p \le n+1$. That is exactly the saturation mechanism: the local factor freezes at $1 - 1/p$ and stops moving.

Slope −1 — Goldbach

$\mathcal{H} = \{0, N\}$, since $r$ and $N-r$ must both be prime. So $\nu_p = 2$ — unless $p \mid N$, when the two offsets collide and $\nu_p = 1$. A collision, again, just a different cause.

Feed $\nu_p$ into the Hardy–Littlewood singular series $\mathfrak{S} = \prod_p \frac{1 - \nu_p/p}{(1 - 1/p)^2}$ and the $\{0,N\}$ case collapses:

$$ \mathfrak{S}(N) \;=\; 2\,C_2 \prod_{\substack{p \mid N \\ p > 2}} \frac{p-1}{p-2}, \qquad C_2 = \prod_{p>2}\left(1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2}\right) \approx 0.6601618158 $$

The $p = 2$ collision supplies the leading $2$. Every odd prime dividing $N$ supplies one $\frac{p-1}{p-2}$. Nothing was added — the correction is the collision count, read off the same rule that gives $\min(n+1,p)$.

Slope $+1$, width 1, offset $g$: the strip density must equal $\varphi(g)/g$. Slope $-1$, width 1, offset $N$: the point count must equal $\varphi(N)$ exactly — this is the count behind Strip Arithmetic I's central claim, that the sum strip is fully coprime iff $\varphi(N) = N-1$, i.e. iff $N$ is prime.

gcd distribution — counts, ratios, and the exact form

Over the square $[1,n]^2$, the number of ordered pairs with $\gcd(r,M)=d$ is exactly $\Phi(\lfloor n/d \rfloor)$, where $\Phi(m) = 2\sum_{k\le m}\varphi(k) - 1$ counts the coprime pairs in $[1,m]^2$. Two identities fall out and both are checked live below: $\sum_d \Phi(\lfloor n/d\rfloor) = n^2$ exactly, and $\Phi(n)/n^2 \to 6/\pi^2$.

§3 · The cometDoes νp actually predict anything?

The Goldbach comet plots $r(N)$, the number of ordered prime pairs summing to $N$, against $N$. It famously splits into bands. The bands are $\mathfrak{S}(N)$ — dividing by it collapses them into a single curve. If the residue count were the wrong idea, that would not happen.

§4 · LimitsWhat is and isn't established

  • Exact. $\varphi(g)/g$ on the $+1$ diagonal and $\varphi(N)$ on the $-1$ anti-diagonal are elementary identities, checked here by direct enumeration, not asymptotics.
  • Exact. $\nu_p = \min(n+1,p)$ for consecutive blocks, and $\nu_p \in \{1,2\}$ for $\{0,N\}$. Both are finite residue counts and both are verified above.
  • Exact. The algebraic collapse of $\prod_p \frac{1-\nu_p/p}{(1-1/p)^2}$ to $2C_2\prod_{p\mid N,\,p>2}\frac{p-1}{p-2}$.
  • Heuristic. That $\mathfrak{S}(N)\,\mathrm{li}_2(N)$ approximates $r(N)$. This is the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture. It is unproven. The comet shows it fits; fitting is not proof.
  • Not claimed. Any progress on Goldbach. The lattice sees coprimality; primality is strictly harder and nothing here bridges that.
Normalisation note

$C(n)$ uses local factors $1 - \nu_p/p^2$ because it averages over block anchors — it is a density of integers. The singular series uses $\frac{1-\nu_p/p}{(1-1/p)^2}$ because it compares against a prime-density baseline. Different normalisations of the same $\nu_p$. Conflating them would be an error; the shared object is the residue count, not the Euler factor.
